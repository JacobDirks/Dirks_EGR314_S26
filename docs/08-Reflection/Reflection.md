## **Review of Module's Success**

I successfully integrated an actuator with spi control, several surface mount components, switching regulators, and UART communication as well as a few others I can't recall currently. However, I failed at keeping all my parts surface mount and using only the pcb as I ended up with breakout boards.

## **Microcontroller Startup**

* Learn the ESP version quickly or find something that will tell you even if it is sitting there and counting the pins on the package. Use that information to plan pin outs better and search for diagrams with restricted pins highlighted.
* Learn the coding platform by doing small scripts outside of the project to develop skills in the language and build progress towards your end goal in the project.
* Sometimes it is just adding extra capacitance and sometimes you short it by adding too much solder paste.

## Lessons Learned

1. ESD protection is not something to snicker about after it has bitten you several times in the same project.
2. Code early and when discussing a protocol there isn't a detail that is too small or silly enough to avoid discussing.
3. Test communication protocols with your team early and often.
4. Build in jumper points to protect your circuit while developing it and use those as test points to ease the footprint issues.
5. Isolate everything that could break because somehow it will break and you don't want to pull everything off again.
6. Don't trust just the datasheet, you need to cooperate the story with something else.
7. Don't trust the footprint off the bat because who knows if they planned for pins that are smaller than the other ones or if an engineer made a mistake.
8. Record the successes because if you don't when you blink the success will vanish and inevitably it will be right before demonstration.
9. If you're known for being good at something people who aren't good and don't want to learn how to do it will ask you incessantly. Therefore, be just good enough to be noticed and have it done early but pretend you're struggling more than you are if you value your workflow's peace.
10. Breathe, it will workout or it won't but the people around you are not there trying to fail you or trying to inhibit you. Find those that support you and breathe life back into each other when it's needed.

## **Recommendations for Future Students**

1. Datasheets and footprints can lie just as much as custom made materials.
2. Don't rely on AI to the point you don't automatically know where something is in your code. It is really irritating if you ask someone for help and don't know your own code.
3. Practice coding outside of class, its not in the course work well enough for you to be proficient so you need to practice on your own. Github has repos on repos of project ideas.
4. Don't sacrifice your sleep to get something done past 3 am. Your brain dies the next day and caffeine is way too addictive to use lightly at night.
5. Remember failure in the classroom may seem large but in comparison to the real world, failing in the classroom is miniscule as the real world either incurs financial ruin, jail time, or loss of life and limb for yourself or others. Take the time learn it right.
6. This class depends on your ability to plan ahead without being told what to expect. Do NOT procrastinate everything. Get it done early or watch your grade suffer.
